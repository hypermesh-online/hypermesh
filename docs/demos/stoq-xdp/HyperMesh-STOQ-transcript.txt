HyperMesh engineering demo | A STOQ exchange observed by Linux XDP
45-second captioned replay of captured output (not a live screen recording)

00:00–00:07  Can Linux see a HyperMesh message?
A client sends a fresh message through HyperMesh's STOQ transport. The experiment
checks the application's byte-for-byte echo and a separate observation from the
Linux kernel. The footage presents one independently repeated run in an isolated
virtual machine.

00:07–00:15  One guest. Two ports.
STOQ used QUIC over IPv6 loopback in a Linux KVM guest. The test harness used
libbpf to attach the project's XDP program. Port 9292 is the target and port
9293 is the comparison.

00:15–00:25  Target: 21 to 23.
The server received the client's fresh 41-byte message and echoed it. Both
sides checked the bytes. The program's port-9292 packet count was 21 after the
handshake and 23 after the echoed payload.

00:25–00:35  Comparison: 25 to 25.
A second, independent, 41-byte STOQ echo succeeded on port 9293. From just
after that handshake until just after its echo, the port-9292 count stayed
at 25; there was no port-9293 entry. These are packet counts; QUIC ACKs and
other control frames may contribute. They are not payload-byte counters.

00:35–00:45  What this proves.
The kernel saw actual STOQ traffic on the intended port. The program detached
and the post-detach query returned ID 0. The test did not demonstrate production-
managed acceleration, a public alpha, two independently installed nodes, or
Proof-of-State authorization of a distributed Catalog-backed BlockMatrix asset.
